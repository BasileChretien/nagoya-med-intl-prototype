The Office of International Affairs is responsible for international matters at Nagoya University Graduate School of Medicine and School of Medicine. It works together with the Department of International Medical Education. The school's aim is to be a hub and a pioneer for collaboration among universities around the world, to find new possibilities for international development, and to foster global citizenship among its faculty and students.

## Three pillars

The office promotes three kinds of work. They aim to produce people who can compete internationally and create new values and ideas.

**Education.** The office builds international literacy and an understanding of other cultures. It offers special lectures and practical training in English, supports study abroad and promotes international education.

**Strategy.** The office plans the school's international strategy, including its international visibility and its place in university rankings. It promotes international co-authorship and joint research, and strategic exchange of people.

**Support.** The office plans international cultural exchange, supports alumni and other networks of people, and helps with internationalization and international medical partnerships.

## Four missions of the unit

The unit's missions are the following.

1. To promote international joint research by the Graduate School of Medicine.
2. To support international students, to teach medical English to undergraduates, to arrange clinical training abroad and to develop an international perspective.
3. To promote international joint education in master's and doctoral programs, such as the joint degree programmes.
4. To provide international services at Nagoya University Hospital, such as support for foreign patients and English versions of hospital documents.

The unit also compares education and hospital internationalization at universities in Japan and abroad, so that the school can respond to what society expects. A fifth area, the Cancer Immune Therapy Research Center, develops treatments for intractable cancer. The head of the office also directs it.

## History

The office began in 1991 as the International Exchange Office. The first student was sent abroad for a clinical clerkship in 1993, to the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. The Department of International Medical Education was created in 2018.
