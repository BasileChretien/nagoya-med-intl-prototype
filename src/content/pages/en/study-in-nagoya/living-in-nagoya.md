This page collects the practical information for students who are coming to Nagoya or already studying here. The Office of International Affairs is your first contact for any question about life and study.

## Support from the office

The office gives advice and guidance on your study, your research and your daily life at Nagoya University. You are welcome to visit. It also shares information on tuition exemptions, scholarships, housing and events on its website and through the mailing list of the Foreign Students Association (FSA).

The office supports the FSA and passes student requests to the university. It also holds a graduation party for international students who finish their studies. The student circle MDEC (MeiDai Exchange Club) welcomes international students to the School of Medicine, and the office works with it to help them take part in activities with confidence.

## Housing

Clinical clerkship students can stay in a dormitory when there are vacancies. Ask the office when you apply.

Nagoya University runs several international residences, among them International Ohmeikan, International Residence Daiko and International Residence Yamate. They offer furnished single rooms with shared areas. Places are limited, so many students rent their own room. The university's [housing page](https://en.nagoya-u.ac.jp/campus/life/housing/) and the Living Guide's [student dormitories page](https://life.gmc.nagoya-u.ac.jp/en/arrival/residence/) describe each residence.

## What it costs to live here

- **University residence.** One clerkship student who stayed in the Yamate international residence in 2024 paid about 26,000 yen a month in rent, plus 3,000 to 4,000 yen for electricity. The same report says that a stay of less than a month pays no electricity ([read the report](../stories/clerkship-2024-report180)).
- **Private room.** A single room in Nagoya usually costs 35,000 to 60,000 yen a month, according to the university's housing page.
- **Fees and insurance.** Tuition, waivers and insurance are on the [clinical clerkship](clinical-clerkship) and [research training](research-training) pages.
- **Food, transport and other costs.** Ask the office if you need an estimate for your budget.

## Visa

If you live abroad and come to Japan as a student, you need a Certificate of Eligibility to apply for a student visa. The university files the application for the certificate. It takes about one month to be issued, so begin early. With the certificate, you then apply for the visa at the nearest Japanese embassy or consulate in your country. The Living Guide's [visa page](https://life.gmc.nagoya-u.ac.jp/en/arrival/visa/) explains each step.

For a clinical clerkship or a research visit of 90 days or less, ask the office which status of entry applies to you before you book your travel.

## On arrival

1. **Visit the Office of International Affairs.** [How to reach the office](../about/access) gives the map and directions. Ask the office what you need for your first day at the hospital or the laboratory.
2. **Residence card.** If you are granted a stay of more than three months, you receive a residence card at the airport (for example at Narita, Haneda, Kansai or Chubu).
3. **Ward office.** If you stay more than three months, register your address at the ward office within 14 days of moving in. You also enrol there in National Health Insurance and, if you are 20 or older, in the National Pension System. See [procedures at the ward offices](https://life.gmc.nagoya-u.ac.jp/en/arrival/ward-office/).
4. **Insurance.** NUPACE students pay monthly premiums to Japan National Health Insurance and buy Student Personal Liability Insurance from the Nagoya University Co-operative. Other visiting students keep the health insurance they showed when they applied.
5. **Emergencies.** Call 119 for an ambulance or a fire engine. Ambulances run 24 hours a day and are free. The Living Guide's [medical and health care page](https://life.gmc.nagoya-u.ac.jp/en/life-in-japan/health/) explains how to find a hospital or clinic that speaks your language.

## Insurance and health care

Students who join through NUPACE pay monthly premiums to Japan National Health Insurance and buy Student Personal Liability Insurance from the Nagoya University Co-operative. Other visiting students need their own health insurance that covers the stay, and show proof when they apply. Clinical clerkship students also need malpractice insurance. The pages on clinical clerkship and research training give the details, and the [FAQ](faq) lists the vaccinations you need.

## If your family comes with you

Your spouse and the children you support can join you with the "Dependent" status of residence, if you have a stable income to support them. You apply for a Certificate of Eligibility for them, and they apply for a visa at a Japanese embassy or consulate. Within 14 days of moving in, they register at the ward office. For day care, contact the ward office of the area where you live. Children of foreign nationality can attend public elementary and junior high schools in Nagoya.

The Living Guide's [Family page](https://life.gmc.nagoya-u.ac.jp/en/life-in-japan/family/) explains each step. These rules are written for students who hold a status of residence, so ask the office whether they apply to your stay.

## Japanese lessons

The university runs a Japanese language course for foreign students at Tsurumai Campus. It teaches basic Japanese skills. The first term runs from April to July and the second from October to February.

- Beginner and intermediate course: every Thursday, 9:00 to 10:30.
- Intermediate and advanced course: every Monday, 9:00 to 10:30.

Classes take place in seminar room 101, on the first floor of the Building for Medical Research. To apply, contact the International Student Service of the Student Affairs Division. If you do not know how to reach it, ask the Office of International Affairs. The university also runs a [short-term Japanese language programme](https://jled.lec.nagoya-u.ac.jp/ja/nustep/).
