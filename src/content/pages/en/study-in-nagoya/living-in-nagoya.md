This page collects the practical information for students who are coming to Nagoya or already studying here. The Office of International Affairs is your first contact for any question about life and study.

## Support from the office

The office gives advice and guidance on your study, your research and your daily life at Nagoya University. You are welcome to visit. It also shares information on tuition exemptions, scholarships, housing and events on its website and through the mailing list of the Foreign Students Association (FSA).

The office supports the FSA and passes student requests to the university. It also holds a graduation party for international students who finish their studies. The student circle MDEC (MeiDai Exchange Club) welcomes international students to the School of Medicine, and the office works with it to help them take part in activities with confidence.

## Housing

Clinical clerkship students can stay in a dormitory when there are vacancies. Ask the office when you apply. The office website also links to a housing guide, a family guide and the Nagoya Living Guide for current students.

## Visa

If you live abroad and come to Japan as a student, you need a Certificate of Eligibility to apply for a student visa. The university files the application for the certificate. It takes about one month to be issued, so begin early. With the certificate, you then apply for the visa at the nearest Japanese embassy or consulate in your country.

## Insurance and health care

Students who join through NUPACE pay monthly premiums to Japan National Health Insurance and buy Student Personal Liability Insurance from the Nagoya University Co-operative. Other visiting students need their own health insurance that covers the stay, and show proof when they apply. Clinical clerkship students also need malpractice insurance. The pages on clinical clerkship and research training give the details.

## Japanese lessons

The university runs a Japanese language course for foreign students at Tsurumai Campus. It teaches basic Japanese skills. The first term runs from April to July and the second from October to February.

- Beginner and intermediate course: every Thursday, 9:00 to 10:30.
- Intermediate and advanced course: every Monday, 9:00 to 10:30.

Classes take place in seminar room 101, on the first floor of the Building for Medical Research. To apply, contact the International Student Service of the Student Affairs Division.

## Getting to the campus

The School of Medicine and the Office of International Affairs are at 65 Tsurumai-cho, Showa-ku, Nagoya 466-8550, Japan. A map from Tsurumai station to the office is available from the office.
