Medical students of Nagoya University can study abroad in three ways: through a university program, through certified study abroad (認定留学), or as a private student (私費留学). The Office of International Affairs gives advice on all three.

## Main routes

University programs send you to partner universities and similar institutions. The main ones are:

- [Clinical electives abroad](clinical-electives). You train abroad for up to three months, between January of the fifth year and April of the sixth year.
- [Research abroad](research-abroad). You take part in basic-science research at a university or research institute abroad.
- [Short programs](short-programmes). These include summer camps, entrepreneurship programs, a short clerkship in the UK and an exchange with Cambridge.
- [Medical English classes and training](medical-english). These are classes at Nagoya and English courses abroad.

You can also go abroad outside the university programs, as a certified or private student. The office gives advice and information in this case too.

## How the office helps

You can ask for advice at any time. The study periods and programs open to you are limited, and an application takes effort, so please come early.

- Study-abroad advice, to find a program that fits your goals and English level.
- Information on [scholarships](scholarships), both inside and outside the university.
- Support for language study. The office runs preparation courses for language tests and lends reference books.
- Study-abroad seminars, which help you think about life abroad and afterwards and settle your worries.
- Pre-departure training in English on Saturdays, for students going on clinical clerkships.
- A report meeting each August, where returned students present by university.

A long stay abroad is hard to fit into the medical curriculum. Credits cannot be transferred, so you would need to take a leave of absence.

Common questions are answered in the [FAQ](faq). The contact box on this page shows how to reach the office.
