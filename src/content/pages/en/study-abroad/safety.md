The Office of International Affairs prepares you before you leave and stays in touch with you during your stay. This page explains what the office does, what you arrange yourself, and where parents can find the essentials.

## Before you leave

The office holds a crisis-management seminar for students who plan to study abroad. Using real cases, it explains how to avoid risks abroad, what to do when you face one, and how to take out insurance for your stay. It is held as needed, and the office contacts the students concerned.

Students going on a clinical clerkship abroad also take pre-departure training: they learn clinical skills in English on Saturdays. See [clinical electives abroad](clinical-electives).

Check the safety level of your destination on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs [overseas safety website](https://www.anzen.mofa.go.jp/) (in Japanese).

Register your trip on the ministry's [Tabireg](https://www.ezairyu.mofa.go.jp/tabireg/) service (in Japanese). You then receive safety information from the embassy or consulate at your destination from before departure until the end of the trip. Your family in Japan can register their email address too. If you stay abroad for three months or more, the Passport Act requires a residence registration (在留届), which you can submit from three months before departure.

## Insurance

The crisis-management seminar covers insurance during your stay. Ask the office which insurance your program requires before you book your flights. For the basic-science placement abroad, the Shibahara Fund support also covers insurance costs. See [research abroad](research-abroad).

## During your stay

At the host university, you train with the support of its staff. The office keeps in contact with you to look after your safety and health. For the clinical clerkship abroad, a dedicated faculty member supports you from preparation to return.

## In an emergency

If you fall ill, are injured or are caught in an incident abroad, contact the Office of International Affairs. Its email address and telephone number are on the [contact page](../about/contact). Before you leave, ask the office how to reach it outside office hours, and keep that number with you.

## If a destination changes or is cancelled

Destinations can change from year to year, so check the latest call. In 2017, some universities stopped accepting students at short notice. Some students had to change their plans, for example by training at another university, and all of them came home safely.

The JASSO scholarship for agreement-based study abroad does not, in principle, support stays in regions at level 2 or higher on the ministry's overseas safety website.

## For parents

If your son or daughter plans to study abroad, these pages answer the main questions. The [study abroad overview](index) lists every program, and the [clinical electives](clinical-electives) page gives the dates of the clerkship abroad. For costs and support, see [scholarships](scholarships) and the cost question in the [FAQ](faq).

This page covers safety. You can also register your own email address on Tabireg to receive the same safety information as your child. The Office of International Affairs is on the [contact page](../about/contact).
