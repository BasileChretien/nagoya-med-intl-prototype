Nagoya University School of Medicine was a pioneer of overseas clinical clerkships. In 1993 it sent its first students to the University of North Carolina. Since then the list of partners has grown to include universities in the United States, Europe, Australia and Asia.

## Who and when

You do the clerkship abroad during Clinical Clerkship II in the final year, for up to three months. The dispatch period and the expected timing of the next call are in the summary at the top of this page. Calls are posted on TACT and the notice board.

## Content and credit

The training must meet the requirements of the Nagoya clinical clerkship. Credit is recognized at Nagoya, so you do not need to make up any clerkship. Abroad, you have the same rights as local students and can follow the same training.

## Costs and support

Tuition is normally waived under the agreements, but a few universities charge it. Some provide a dormitory, and at others you must find housing yourself.

The Keiichi Shibahara Fund offers up to JPY 1.5 million for up to 12 students a year, adjusted by destination and period. Other scholarships may also apply. See [scholarships](scholarships).

## Requirements and timeline

### What the call asks for

Each year's call, posted on TACT and the notice board, sets the English test and minimum score, the documents to submit and the selection method. Ask the study-abroad adviser for the current requirements before May of your fifth year.

### Steps

1. Before you apply, go to the report meeting of returned students, held around August. It is open to students who want to go the following year and to younger students.
2. Apply to the call on TACT and the notice board by its deadline. When to expect it is in the summary at the top of this page.
3. Students are selected within the university.
4. Before departure, you take clinical-skills training in English on Saturdays and a crisis-management seminar.
5. You train abroad for up to three months, within the dispatch period shown in the summary.
6. You come home and report on your stay. Students of the 2023 and 2024 programs came home in May. The report meetings were held on 3 August 2023, 5 August 2024 and 31 July 2025.

## Preparation and safety

A dedicated faculty member supports you. Before you leave, you take training in the clinical skills you will need, taught in English on Saturdays by physicians with experience abroad. A crisis-management seminar is held before departure, and the office stays in touch with you during your stay. Insurance, emergencies and a summary for parents are on [safety, insurance and emergencies](safety).

## After you return

A report meeting is held around August each year, where you can ask returned students about each university. Recent dispatches reached 15 universities in 2025, and 13 students went in each of 2024 and 2023.

Students who trained abroad wrote reports on each university. Read them on [student stories](../stories).

The Frontier-kai (フロンティア会) is the alumni association of Nagoya students who trained abroad. Students founded it in 1998 to share information and train younger students. Ask the office how to join.

Talk to the study-abroad adviser at the office well before you apply.
