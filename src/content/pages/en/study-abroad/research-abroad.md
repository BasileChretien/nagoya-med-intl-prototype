If you are interested in research, two programs let you learn basic medical science at a university or research institute abroad. Both are supported by the Keiichi Shibahara Fund.

## Basic medical seminar abroad

This program is for third-year students. You learn research methods in basic medicine and the latest knowledge, and you see a research environment abroad, which can help you plan a career as a researcher.

The stay lasts up to six months. The fund offers up to JPY 3 million, adjusted by destination and period, and is stated to support 2 students a year.

## Placement in basic-science departments abroad

Fifth- and sixth-year students can do part of the elective clerkship in a basic-science department at a research institute abroad. The aim is to train medical researchers who can work internationally, and the program is for students with strong research motivation. Through the research you see the front line of medicine and medical research abroad.

The stay lasts up to three months. The fund offers up to JPY 1.5 million for 2 students a year, adjusted by destination and period. It covers travel, living costs, tuition, bench fees, insurance and similar costs.

## How to apply

Details are announced on TACT, and briefings are held from time to time. No dates are published, so contact the Office of International Affairs early if you are interested.

## Graduate students and young researchers

Doctoral students and young researchers have a separate route. The scholar exchange with the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill offers a research stay of up to one year. Eligibility, places, funding and how to apply are on [scholar exchange with UNC](../degrees/unc-scholar-exchange). Other options for doctoral students are listed on the [study abroad overview](index).

The [scholarships](scholarships) page describes the Shibahara Fund as a whole. Before you leave, read [safety, insurance and emergencies](safety).
