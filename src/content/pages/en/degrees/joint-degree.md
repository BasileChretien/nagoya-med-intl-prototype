In a joint degree programme, two universities build one educational programme together. You work on a single research project under two supervisors, one at each university. When you finish, the two universities award one degree in their joint names. You complete it within the usual four-year doctoral course, and a stay of at least 12 months at the partner university is guaranteed.

## Joint degree and double degree

A joint degree is one degree conferred jointly by the partner universities after you complete a single joint programme. A double degree consists of separate degrees from each university, each awarded when you meet that university's requirements.

Because of this, a double degree normally needs two different theses. In the joint degree programmes with Adelaide and Lund, one thesis serves for both universities. The final oral examination is held once, at the principal university, with the partner university's examiners and your partner supervisor joining by web conference. Terms such as joint, double and dual degree are not defined in the same way in every country or institution, so check what a programme means by them.

## Credits and the stay abroad

You must earn the credits required for a regular doctoral course, split between courses offered at Nagoya (group A) and at the partner university (group B). The total is 30 credits for Adelaide and Freiburg and 48 credits for Lund (students admitted from 1 January 2024). Because both universities supervise your experiments together, the effort is lighter than completing two separate doctorates.

The stay abroad is at least 12 months and normally starts from the second year. The exact period is agreed among you, your main supervisor and your partner supervisor, according to the progress of the project.

## The degree

The degree is a Doctor of Philosophy (in Medicine). The diploma carries a note that it is conferred as a single joint degree under a jointly established programme. It is the proof of your international experience.

## History

Japan allowed joint degrees with foreign universities after the university establishment standards were revised in November 2014. The first programme in Japan was the one with the University of Adelaide, approved by the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) and established on 15 June 2015. Programmes with Lund University (November 2016) and the University of Freiburg (June 2018) followed. The first joint degree in Japan was conferred on 9 March 2020.

## Support

You pay admission and tuition fees only to Nagoya University, and the partner university's tuition is waived. A travel allowance and a monthly research allowance for the stay abroad are available. The amounts and conditions are on [Admission, fees and support](admission).
