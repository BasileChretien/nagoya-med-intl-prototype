The Graduate School of Medicine offers three kinds of programme for doctoral students and young researchers who want to spend a long period of research at a university abroad. They differ in the degree you receive and in how long you stay.

## The three types

**Joint degree.** You follow one jointly designed doctoral programme with one research project and two supervisors, one at each university. At the end you receive a single degree issued in the names of both universities. You stay at the partner university for at least 12 months within the usual four years. Nagoya University offers joint degrees with Adelaide University, Lund University and the University of Freiburg. See [Joint degree programmes](joint-degree).

**Double degree.** You enrol in the doctoral programmes of both universities and receive a separate degree from each. You write two dissertations with different content. Nagoya University offers this with The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK). See [Double degree with CUHK](cuhk-double-degree).

**Scholar exchange.** You carry out research in a laboratory of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC) for three months to one year, and receive a certificate of completion. You do not receive a UNC degree, and the results can become part of your own doctoral dissertation. See [Scholar exchange with UNC](unc-scholar-exchange).

## Which one suits you

Choose a joint degree if you want an internationally recognised single degree and a stay of a year or more with a partner supervisor. Choose the double degree if you want a doctorate from each of two universities and are ready to write two dissertations. Choose the scholar exchange if you want a shorter stay, or if you are a young faculty member or researcher rather than a degree candidate.
