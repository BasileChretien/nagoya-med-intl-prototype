GAME is a network for the development of medical education and research. It was founded in 2017 by the medical schools of nine universities, at the invitation of the dean of medicine at The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK). Only medical schools belong to it, and each member takes charge of a joint project. Nagoya University has been a member since the start. Hideki Kasuya and Itzel Bustos lead its work at Nagoya. The alliance also has its own website at [game-med.net](https://www.game-med.net).

## Members

The University of Alberta left in 2020, which leaves eight member schools: Nagoya University and the seven partners listed below.

## Priority projects

Each project is run by one member school.

| International Online Barrett's Esophagus Registry (IOBER) | University of Bologna |
| Transnational Educational Initiative (TEI) | Korea University |

PhD4GAME is a further priority. It adds a layer of support for research between member schools through shared PhD training, with a focus on exchanges between two or three institutions.

## Activities for students

**TEI summer camp.** TEI is led by Korea University and is meant mainly for undergraduate and graduate medical students. Member schools hold a summer camp each year. Nagoya hosted the first camp in 2019. Eighteen students from seven member universities spent five days on disaster medicine and medical professionalism, including a two-day visit to Fukushima. The second camp was held in Bologna in 2020 and the third in 2021 at Monash University, online from 26 to 29 July.

**Online clinical case discussion.** Started within the TEI project in November and December 2020, it brings together senior medical students from member schools. Students present clinical cases under the guidance of their professors and compare how health systems and health insurance work in each country. The second series ran every Tuesday from 25 May to 29 June 2021, led by CUHK, Korea University, LMU Munich, Monash and Nagoya, with Bologna as a guest. Twenty-three students from six universities took part. A third series of seven sessions was planned for 24 May to 5 July 2022. The discussion is free of charge, and participants receive a certificate of attendance.

## Annual meeting

Members take turns to host the annual meeting. Nagoya hosted the sixth meeting on 3 and 4 November 2022, the first in person for three years, with more than 20 delegates from the member schools.

## Taking part

Only medical schools belong to GAME. The old pages do not describe how a new school joins. A medical school that wants to take part in a project, or to reach a project's lead at Nagoya, can write to the office.
